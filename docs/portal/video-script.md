# The 180-second video: story, script and production plan

> **Owner:** Presentation / Communication Lead. **Version 2.1 (18 Nov 2026):** built around the team leader's story (IBD rising and little known in our region, no cure and no known cause, triggers that differ per person, research and awareness), then checked against the portal's **YouTube Video Guidance** (section 2). Every fact in the narration is checked against a published source (section 7).

This one video decides whether we reach the finals (guide §9.2). It is scored segment by segment, so the structure below follows the guide's five fixed segments to the second. The same story becomes our 3-minute live pitch on 26 December.

---

## 1. The story in one breath

> **Nusrat has Crohn's disease, a condition most people in Bangladesh have never heard of, with no cure, no known cause and triggers that are different for every patient. HappyGut learns *her* triggers from her own diary and tells her, before she eats, what is likely to set her off. And if thousands of patients choose to share their anonymised patterns, our region finally gets the data that IBD research here is missing.**

Three layers, in this order, so judges feel it before they analyse it:
1. **One person** (Nusrat at a wedding, unsure what she can eat). This is the emotional hook.
2. **One product** that solves her problem today, shown working. This is the proof.
3. **One bigger mission**: awareness in Bangladesh and data for research, so Bangladesh contributes to a global question. This is the vision.

**Open and close on the same plate.** The video starts with Nusrat afraid of the wedding plate and ends with her eating from it with confidence. That callback is what people remember.

## 3. The script

**Narration:** English, calm and warm, about 150 words a minute, so no more than about 2.5 words a second (the script below is 390 words). English reaches the international and NRB judges; Bangla is everywhere else: Nusrat speaks Bangla, the app is shown in Bangla, and the closing line is in both.

**On-screen text:** short, large, white on dark green (`#0A4237`) or on a blurred background. Sources in small grey text at the bottom (section 7).

**Segment labels:** at the start of each segment, show a small label in the top-left corner for about 3 seconds: **1 · Problem**, **2 · Solution**, **3 · Demo**, **4 · AI approach**, **5 · Impact**. Evaluators score each segment separately, so make it easy for them to see where each one starts.

## 4. Production plan

### Who does what

| Job | Person (fill in) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Director and editor | Presentation Lead | Owns timing and the final cut |
| Narrator | the clearest English speaker on the team | **A team member's own voice, never text-to-speech**: evaluators score energy, confidence and ownership. Smile while reading; it is audible. Record in a quiet room, phone 20 cm from the mouth, inside a wardrobe or under a blanket if the room echoes |
| Nusrat (on camera) | a teammate | Ideally a woman on the team (also a guide bonus, §13.3) |
| App recording | Frontend Developer | Knows the app; records each demo shot several times |
| Facts and wording check | Business Analyst | Checks every number against section 7 before export |

### Free tools
- **Screen recording on a phone:** Android's built-in screen recorder (Quick Settings), at 1080p. If the app isn't deployed yet, use Chrome on a laptop with device mode at 390 × 844 and **OBS Studio** to record.
- **Live action:** any recent phone, horizontal, 1080p 30 fps, near a window for soft light.
- **Voice:** phone voice recorder, then **Audacity** (Noise Reduction, Normalize to −1 dB).
- **Editing:** **CapCut** (desktop, quickest) or **DaVinci Resolve** (free, more control). Export 1920 × 1080, 30 fps, H.264 MP4.
- **Text cards and diagram:** **Canva** (free), 1920 × 1080, fonts Anek Latin / Anek Bangla to match the app.
- **Music:** one quiet, hopeful track from the **YouTube Audio Library** (no attribution needed tracks), at about −24 dB under the voice.

### Before recording the app
1. Use the **deployed** app if it is ready (judges will open the same link); otherwise the local app with a Gemini key in `.env`.
2. Sign in as `nusrat@happygut.test`. In Settings, tap **Restore original records** before every take so the diary for today is empty.
3. Switch the app to **বাংলা**. Close every other notification on the phone (Do Not Disturb).
4. For the photo shot, buy a real plate of kacchi, borhani and firni. Try the photo check 2–3 times first. If the model names the dishes reliably, use the photo; if not, type the dishes in Bangla. **Never fake or edit a result.**
5. Record each demo shot separately (check, evidence, diary, patterns, My Gut, Today). Long takes are fine; the edit cuts them.

### Edit order
1. Lay the narration first and cut it to exactly 3:00.
2. Put pictures under the words, segment by segment.
3. Speed up typing and scrolling (1.5–2×); never speed up the verdict appearing.
4. Add the captions, number cards and sources.
5. Add subtitles for **all** speech (many people watch muted). YouTube can auto-generate; correct them by hand.
6. Mix: voice clear, music low, no music under Nusrat's Bangla line.

## 6. Rules the video must follow (check before export)

- [ ] Exactly 3:00 or just under; each segment within its time window
- [ ] Bangla visible or audible in at least three places (Nusrat's line, the app, the closing line)
- [ ] "Real app · sample patient records" caption during the whole demo minute
- [ ] Every number matches section 7 and the evaluation (`evals/RESULTS.md`); "simulated" is said with the 72%
- [ ] The research part uses **"could", "if patients choose to share", "anonymised"**: it is our next step, not a feature that exists today
- [ ] No feature shown that the app doesn't have (no voice input, wearables, AI-written meal plans, doctor dashboard)
- [ ] Never "safe", "cure", "causes", "predicts flares", "clinically validated" or "doctor-approved"
- [ ] Subtitles on all speech
- [ ] Last frame: team name, live link, and (if space) the five names with roles
- [ ] The same file on YouTube and Facebook
- [ ] YouTube visibility is **Public** or **Unlisted**, never Private. Paste the full link (`https://youtu.be/...`) into the portal, then open it in a private browser window to check it plays without signing in
- [ ] Segment labels appear at 0:00, 0:30, 1:00, 2:00 and 2:30
- [ ] A team member's own voice; the team is on screen at the end

**Why the care:** the guide disqualifies "fabricated demos" and "fake or simulated results" (§20.4) and asks us not to misrepresent technical capabilities (§20.9). Saying "sample patient" and "simulated" out loud turns a risk into a sign of integrity, which is part of the Responsible AI score.

## 8. After the video: the same story, live

The finals (26 Dec) give each team 3 minutes of pitch in a 5-minute slot, then judges' questions (guide §9.3). Keep the same five segments, show the app live from a phone, and prepare answers to:
- "Is the data real?" → sample records in the demo, simulated cohort for the evaluation, and the plan for a consented pilot.
- "Why not just ChatGPT?" → the AI doesn't decide; a tested engine does, with citations, the same answer every time, and warning signs handled first.
- "How do you make money?" → clinic licence first, patient-support programmes, freemium (`docs/01-strategy.md` §6).
- "How does research work without breaking privacy?" → opt-in, anonymised, aggregate statistics only; names removed before any AI call; patients own their records.
